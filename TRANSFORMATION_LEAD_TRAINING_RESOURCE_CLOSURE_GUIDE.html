4. Management-purpose alignment review

The resource set now teaches that the system’s real purpose is to help leaders:

understand
test
decide
govern
revise
learn

The six management-intention questions are visible and operationalized:

Management-intention questionCoverage assessment
What is structurally happening in the organization?Covered through diagnosis, structural signals, scorecards, evidence, and review-pack preparation.
What are people experiencing?Covered through survey/evidence interpretation, human experience worksheets, dignity, voice, trust, and respect doctrine.
Where do stated values, trust signals, or symbolic claims diverge from actual structural conditions?Covered through symbolic-structural gap analysis and presentational/value-realising performance distinction.
Which recommendations should be preserved, reformed, replaced, watched, accepted, or deferred?Covered as a major recommendation-gating judgment point.
Are interventions producing measurable improvement through pilots and KPIs?Covered through pilot/KPI linkage, execution feedback, and proposed-versus-demonstrated change.
What should governance approve, block, reopen, qualify, or escalate?Covered as a major governance-gating judgment point.

9. Learning orientation review

The training set teaches that recommendations, decisions, and pilots can be:

  • validated;
  • contradicted;
  • superseded;
  • reopened.

It also teaches:

  • an accepted recommendation is not evidence of improvement;
  • pilots and KPIs distinguish proposed change from demonstrated change;
  • review history and continuity prevent findings from disappearing after meetings.

Review result:

Learning orientation is adequately preserved for Transformation Lead training.

12. How to use the worked example

Use:

user/WORKED_TRAINING_EXAMPLE_ASSESSMENT_CYCLE.md

Recommended use:

  1. Teach the six management questions first.
  2. Review the illustrative scenario.
  3. Walk participants through each management question.
  4. Pause at recommendation gates and governance gates.
  5. Ask participants to identify where evidence is weak, contradicted, supportable, or not exercised.
  6. Ask participants to identify presentational-performance risks.
  7. End by preparing or critiquing the review-pack outline.

Do not present the worked example as live application data.

It is an illustrative training scenario.

13. How to use the competency assessment

Use:

user/TRANSFORMATION_LEAD_TRAINING_COMPETENCY_ASSESSMENT.md

Recommended use:

  1. Use after the foundation and applied sessions.
  2. Use again during supervised practice.
  3. Assess qualitatively, not numerically.
  4. Look for judgment, not memorised slogans.
  5. Record evidence, concerns, follow-up practice, and readiness decision.
  6. Reassess if major gaps remain.
